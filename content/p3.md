### How we started — timeline

- **Feb 2024** — Aarav and I watched a video of Atlas (Boston Dynamics) doing parkour. So insane we wanted to build it. Decided to start a club. Purpose of the robot? Space — I love it, and it's Purdue: first man on the moon → first humanoid on the moon. Worked day and night on initial plans, approached professors.
- **Feb 27, 2024** — Club officially approved. Founding team: Aarav (President), me (VP), Aayan (Treasurer), Parth (Ops Head), Ashwath (Marketing Head), Yan Gu (Primary Advisor).
- **Spring 2024** — Branding, sponsorship packets, decks; met Purdue leadership. Onboarded tech leadership: Yash (PM), Katy (Mech Lead), Matina (Elec Lead), Ludwig (Software Lead, joined later).
- **Summer 2024** — Landed AMD, Mouser Electronics, Autodesk, etc. as sponsors. Planned with members + did marketing.
- **Fall 2024** — First info session / callout: **400+ people attended** — insane to see the interest.

1. Walk smoothly in simulations
2. Swim mid-air (same walking strides, mid-air)
3. Taken first steps on the ground; basic walking motions work
4. Built hands and arms separately — hands function (victory sign, yo, etc.)
5. Arms can be mounted on a stand and work; not yet attached to NEMO

### How it started

I went to multiple hackathons and saw they only had hardware tracks with very basic hardware, and barely any mechanical or electrical engineers showing up. So i was like: what if we made one hackathon specifically for them? That's how we came up with the idea of StarkHacks.

Purdue had recently set a world record for the fastest solve of a Rubik's Cube, and I wanted to set a world record for Purdue too. So we combined the two ideas and hosted the world's largest hardware hackathon.

&nbsp;
