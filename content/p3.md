### Fun facts + stories

- **How we got AMD:** Aarav emailed Lisa Su and we got a reply from her. She connected us to Andrew Schmidt, head of the university program at AMD, and since then we've been working with them — they became our top sponsor.
- **How we met top leadership:** As Lisa Su was leaving Loeb after her presidential lecture series with President Chiang, Aayan and I waited for her at the back gate and got a chance to tell her all about HRC. CTO Mark vouched for us in front of her — really cool moment.
- **How we started HRC:** Aarav and I were watching a video of Atlas (Boston Dynamics) doing parkour. It was so insane that we wanted to build something like it. We thought of starting a club for it — and what would the purpose of the robot be? I said space because I love it so much, and it's Purdue: first man on the moon → first humanoid on the moon makes sense. We worked day and night on initial plans, approached professors to help start the club. Founding team:
  - Aarav — President
  - Me — VP
  - Aayan — Treasurer
  - Parth — Ops Head
  - Ashwath — Marketing Head
  - Yan Gu — Primary Advisor

  The club was officially approved on **February 27, 2024**. We worked on branding, sponsorship packets, and decks to meet with Purdue leadership over the next two months. Also onboarded the technical leadership: Yash (Project Manager), Katy (Mechanical Lead), Matina (Electrical Lead), Ludwig (Software Lead, joined later). Over summer we landed sponsors like AMD, Mouser Electronics, Autodesk, etc., planned everything with members, and did marketing. Our first info session / callout had **400+ people attend** — insane to see the amount of interest from students.

## **STARKHACKS**

&nbsp;

### How it started

I went to multiple hackathons and saw they only had hardware tracks with very basic hardware — and no mechanical or electrical engineers showing up. So it was like: what if we made one for them? That's how we came up with the idea of StarkHacks.

Purdue had recently set a world record for the fastest solve of a Rubik's Cube, and I wanted to set a world record for Purdue too. So we combined the two ideas and hosted the world's largest hardware hackathon.

&nbsp;

### Sponsors

Got AMD and Ford as co-hosts; Tesla, Microsoft, Dremel, SolidWorks, Anthropic, etc. as sponsors.

&nbsp;

### Stats

- 750+ hackers
- 10+ countries
- 80+ universities

&nbsp;

### Speakers

&nbsp;

### Outcome

People built very cool projects — so happy with how it went. Lots of learnings from the event too.
