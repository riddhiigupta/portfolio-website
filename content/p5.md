# beliefs + recommendations

&nbsp;

<details style="margin:1.4rem 0; border-bottom:1px solid rgba(210,175,110,0.18); padding-bottom:0.5rem;">
<summary style="cursor:pointer; color:rgba(237,232,216,0.95); letter-spacing:0.18em; text-transform:lowercase; font-weight:700; font-size:clamp(0.95rem,1.4vw,1.15rem); padding:0.65rem 0; list-style:none;">▸ learnings</summary>

<p style="font-size:0.85rem; color:rgba(237,232,216,0.7); margin:1rem 0 1.4rem; line-height:1.7;">things i've picked up running clubs, teams, and just trying to figure life out. some are leadership, some are life, some are just how i think.</p>

- You can hire fast and fire fast at a company, but in a club you only have a limited pool of people to work with so you have to take your time
- People work in very different ways so you need to talk to each of them in a way that makes them feel smart.
- You need to have difficult convos no matter what.
- There needs to be transparency about what each person is working on for accountability.
- You need to ensure everyone is working towards one overarching goal instead of smaller goals for their own teams.
- You must be willing to engage in conflict.
- The "one bad apple" thing is very real.
- If you want to reduce dependency on you and ensure longevity long after you're gone, you need to make systems so there's something for everyone to follow.
- Everyone needs to be at par with the vision of the company even if it has to go through serious conflict to reach that stage.
- Try your best to be friends with the people on your teambecause when you're friends with someone you want to work with them.
- If your business is consumer-centric, do your best to not make any major public errors.
- Celebrate with your team after every major win. Non-negotiable.
- Take care of your team. Be understanding of their situation, and have fun with them instead of bossing them around.
- If there's a big sponsor or big interview, take the people who really deserve it to the stage and give them the limelight.
- Feedback sessions with your team ensure you're comfortable and respect each other enough to make changes. Be willing to listen to others' opinions of you and adjust.
- If something is hard, just ask your colleague. It builds trust and it makes them feel smart too.
- Life is genuinely much easier and fun when you're doing what *you* want to do instead of what people want you to do.
- Be more straightforward with people.

</details>

<details style="margin:1.4rem 0; border-bottom:1px solid rgba(210,175,110,0.18); padding-bottom:0.5rem;">
<summary style="cursor:pointer; color:rgba(237,232,216,0.95); letter-spacing:0.18em; text-transform:lowercase; font-weight:700; font-size:clamp(0.95rem,1.4vw,1.15rem); padding:0.65rem 0; list-style:none;">▸ beliefs</summary>

<p style="font-size:0.85rem; color:rgba(237,232,216,0.7); margin:1rem 0 1.4rem; line-height:1.7;">how i see the world and how i try to live in it.</p>

- **Everything in life is an experience.** You could write a piece of code that does the job, but if you have nice visuals and design and a person gets an experience out of it, they remember you because they *felt* something because of you.
- **Your body is the first way people judge your competence before you even speak.** People do think "wow this guy has a sleek physique, takes care of himself" which signals discipline and hard work even before they know anything else about you. Plus people are more likely to favor someone they find attractive.
- **You can turn your life around any time.** I lost my way in high school. Then I came to Purdue and was inspired by what other people had done. Since then I've been all about building, working hard, and now I'm ahead of a lot of people I know who (in my terms) are wasting their lives like I did back then.
- **Be more excited about things in life and try to have as much fun as you can.** I work all day but even then I take 1–2 hours spread out to talk to my friends and hang out. I also just work *with* them because they also work all day so it's fun. Life is genuinely all about being happy, minimize any time spent towards being unhappy.
- **Don't be afraid to try out new things and hate it.** I tried CS, didn't like it. I hang out with engineers all the time, so they used to be like "design ew" — but, I genuinely loved design. I used to be embarrassed about it, then came to accept it as my USP and life is so much better. Same with marketing — I didn't like to be associated with that, but a person who understands tech, has taste, AND can do marketing? That is a superpower, nothing to be embarrassed about. So yeah, you're really cool, just accept your passions and don't be embarrassed of it.
- **Don't be afraid to leave books in the middle.** I start a book, don't find it great because maybe it's not relevant to my situation right now but doesn't mean it's a bad book, just that I read it and didn't find it useful, and now have a better idea of when I'd need it.
- **Every relationship in the world is transactional.** If you have a mentor giving you so much of their time to make you better at something, they see potential in you they want to nurture and that makes them happy or feel like they're investing in something. If you make a friend, either you make them feel smarter, make them smarter, or you're funny and you make them laugh. You give something (or multiple things) and they give something back.
- **Luck surface area** is a very real thing and it's directly related to how many people know you / about you. The more people know what you're doing , the more they'll reach out when an opportunity makes sense, or you'll reach out to them, and grow easily.

</details>
